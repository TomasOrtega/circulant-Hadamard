## Mathematical choices and source review

Read the supplied local primary paper, Section 4 and Lemma 3.3, at
`/private/tmp/circulant-source-paper.txt`. The sign identities are direct
algebra from its normalized evaluations. The polynomial E(P) packages the two
exact quotients used later in the paper; this is explanatory organization,
not a new claimed mathematical result.

- Form the divisions by 2 and 1+i integrally before reduction. Gaussian parity
  follows from the explicit quotient formula for a+bi; it applies coefficient
  by coefficient. The all-ones congruence does not need norm identities or
  oddness. Oddness is used separately when evaluating at one.
- Retain the website's single ring A=Z[i,{zeta_p},1/u] and its quotient by 1+i.
  The norm gives explicit inverses for all evaluated denominators. Explain
  why the quotient is nonzero: the ring generated by the roots is finite free
  over Z, has a maximal ideal above 2, and odd u remains nonzero in its residue
  field. This justifies the existing global-ring presentation; the paper uses
  localization at one maximal ideal instead.
- Prove odd-order rigidity by cancelling a geometric-sum factor in the
  characteristic-zero domain before reducing. No cancellation in a quotient
  with possible zero divisors is used.
- Separate general alternating-product identities from the two-input common
  rotation for u=3. Derive the E ratio equality without dividing by E itself.
  A unit denominator does not make the raw E values equal modulo 1+i, and the
  schematic rotation is not assigned the hypothetical all-ones residues.

The general rigidity proof depends on the previously stated Delta theorem;
this task does not independently reprove that theorem or verify the entire
circulant Hadamard result. The auxiliary facts about finitely generated
abelian groups and maximal ideals are standard algebra inputs stated in the
unit argument.
