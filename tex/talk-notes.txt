STEVEN — IDEAS OF THE PROOF

6. Only orders 1 and 4 — 0:55 (8:25–9:20)
   Define the matrices and show the working 4×4 example. Credit the theorem
   and proof to OpenAI's September 23, 2026 preprint. Our contribution is the
   exposition, website, and visual explanation. The guiding case is n=36.

7. One polynomial records row comparisons — 1:05 (9:20–10:25)
   Polynomial coefficients are signs; product coefficients are cyclic dot
   products. Substituting X=1 makes n a square. For n>1, orthogonal distinct
   rows make n even. The deeper power-of-two descent makes u odd in n=4u².
   Proceed by contradiction with u>1; use u=3 as the illustration.

8. Three evaluations, one circle — 1:15 (10:25–11:40)
   CRT reindexes the positions into a four-cycle and nine-cycle. Group into
   H_0 through H_3 and substitute fourth roots 1, −1, i for Z.
   Sign sums make the halves integral; dividing each factor by two changes
   the norm 36 to 9. For Gaussian coefficients, star conjugates coefficients
   as well as reversing powers. Allowed root inputs put c,d,g on radius three.

9. The Delta theorem — 1:10 (11:40–12:50)
   Delta(f)=f(1)/f(omega) records a relative phase. Magnitudes cancel, but
   magnitude alone is insufficient. Local arithmetic establishes integrality;
   all conjugates have modulus one; Kronecker gives a root of unity; the local
   residue argument makes its order odd. For this field, only 1, omega, omega²
   remain. These values need not individually equal one.

10. Cook up the all-ones polynomial — 1:00 (12:50–13:50)
    E's expression in c,d,g connects it to the phase comparisons. Its expanded
    H expression exposes the coefficients. Divide exactly before reduction;
    i−1 is divisible by 1+i, and both signs reduce to one. At P=1, nine ones
    give E(1) congruent to one.

11. Common rotation — 1:15 (13:50–15:05)
    Compare Delta(d), Delta(g) with Delta(c). The sign relations make these
    comparison roots congruent to one. Odd order then forces R=W=1 exactly.
    Thus all three evaluations, and their linear combination E, share the
    same rotation from P=1 to P=omega. Normalize by c to remove it.
    The needed denominators are units because their norm is the odd number 9.
    The figure illustrates the rotation only; it is not a Hadamard construction.

12. Equal normalized values, incompatible residues — 1:00 (15:05–16:05)
    At one, J=9 and c=±3, leaving residue one. At omega, the three cube-root
    sums cancel, leaving zero. Unit denominators preserve this zero/nonzero
    distinction in a nonzero quotient ring. The equal normalized values
    therefore give a contradiction. Rotation alone never turns a residue zero.

13. General odd u — 1:20 (16:05–17:25)
    Reset J to u² terms. Multiple primes give alternating products of
    evaluations, not one common rotation. Expand the exact product identities,
    cancel in the original domain, and then reduce modulo 1+i to obtain a
    vanishing alternating sum. Nontrivial root inputs kill J; only input one
    survives, leaving ±u congruent to one. Hence u>1 is impossible.
