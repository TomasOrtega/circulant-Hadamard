> **Lemma (Integrality).** *For odd $u>1$ and
> $f(P)f^*(P)=u^2$, the alternating product $\Delta(f)$ is an algebraic
> integer. At every prime above a divisor of $u$, it is a unit with residue $1$.*

## Check possible denominators one prime at a time

Let $I$ be the set of primes dividing $u$, put
$\zeta_S=\prod_{p\in S}\zeta_p$, and let
$B=\mathbb Z[i,\{\zeta_p:p\in I\}]$, the ring containing our evaluated values.
Checking at a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ means allowing every denominator
outside $\mathfrak m$; the resulting ring is written $B_{\mathfrak m}$.
For any fixed $p\in I$, pair a subset not containing $p$ with the subset
obtained by adding $p$. This rewrites the same alternating product as

$$
\Delta(f)=
\prod_{\substack{S\subseteq I\\p\notin S}}
\left(\frac{f(\zeta_S)}{f(\zeta_{S\cup\{p\}})}\right)^{(-1)^{|S|}}.
$$

At every maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ of $B$ containing $p$, Node 4.3
makes each ratio a unit with residue $1$. Products and inverses preserve
both properties. Thus $\Delta(f)$ is a unit of residue $1$ in
$B_{\mathfrak m}$. Repeat this pairing for each prime dividing $u$.

At a maximal ideal containing none of those primes, $u^2$ is a unit.
The identity

$$
f(\zeta_S)\overline{f(\zeta_S)}=u^2
$$

then makes every $f(\zeta_S)$ a unit there, with inverse
$\overline{f(\zeta_S)}/u^2$. Consequently $\Delta(f)$ belongs to
$B_{\mathfrak m}$ for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$.

## Recover a global integral element

Inside the fraction field of a domain, the intersection of all its
localizations at maximal ideals is the original ring. Here is the
denominator argument. If $z\notin B$, the ideal

$$
D_z=\{b\in B:bz\in B\}
$$

is proper. Choose a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ containing it.
Writing $z=a/s$ with $a,s\in B$ and $s\notin\mathfrak m$ would force
$s\in D_z$, a contradiction. Thus $z\notin B_{\mathfrak m}$.

Applying this to $\Delta(f)$ proves $\Delta(f)\in B$. The ring $B$ is
generated by roots of unity, which are algebraic integers, so its elements
are algebraic integers.

This supplies the integrality hypothesis needed for Kronecker's theorem.
Keep the residue-$1$ information too: it will restrict the order of the
resulting root of unity.
