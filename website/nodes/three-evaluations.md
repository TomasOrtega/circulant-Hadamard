## First route: $u=3$, $n=36$

For $Z^4=P^9=1$, define

$$
c(P)=\frac{h(1,P)}2,\qquad d(P)=\frac{h(-1,P)}2,\qquad g(P)=\frac{h(i,P)}2.
$$

Then $cc^*=dd^*=gg^*=9$. Let $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$. At each of $P=1$ and $P=\omega$, all three values have complex absolute value $3$.

Here $f^*(P)=\overline{f(P^{-1})}$ means conjugating the coefficients and inverting the group variable. Thus the norm identity becomes $|f(P)|^2=9$ at a root of unity.

## General statement

Use the Chinese remainder decomposition

$$
\mathbb Z[C_{4u^2}]\cong\mathbb Z[Z,P]/(Z^4-1,\ P^{u^2}-1).
$$

The comma denotes the ideal generated by both relations.

**Input:** $h(Z,P)$ has $4u^2$ sign coefficients and satisfies $h(Z,P)h^*(Z,P)=4u^2$ in this group ring, with $Z^4=P^{u^2}=1$.

**Definitions:**
$$
h(Z,P)=H_0(P)+ZH_1(P)+Z^2H_2(P)+Z^3H_3(P),
$$
$$
c(P)=\frac{h(1,P)}2,\qquad
d(P)=\frac{h(-1,P)}2,\qquad
g(P)=\frac{h(i,P)}2.
$$

**Statement:** These have integer or Gaussian-integer coefficients and
$$
cc^*=dd^*=gg^*=u^2.
$$

**Intuition:** The three independent Fourier evaluations of the four-position cycle are its constant balance, alternating balance, and complex displacement vector. The fourth evaluation $-i$ is conjugate to the third. Dividing by $2$ normalizes the squared norm from $4u^2$ to $u^2$.

**Output:** Three elements satisfying the norm hypothesis of the Δ-theorem.
